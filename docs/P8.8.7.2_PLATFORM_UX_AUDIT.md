## Phase A — UX Audit

---

### 1. Five-Second Test

**What a first-time visitor understands in 5 seconds:**

| Question | Current Answer | Verdict |
|----------|---------------|---------|
| What is this? | "Discover Local Businesses Near You" | ✅ Clear |
| Who is it for? | Customers AND business owners — equal-weight CTAs | ❌ Ambiguous |
| What can I do here? | Browse menus, book tables, order | ✅ Adequate |
| Why should I trust it? | "500+ businesses" pill badge only | ❌ Insufficient |

**Detailed findings:**

The headline communicates the platform identity clearly. The subheadline adds the product verbs (browse, book, order). So far, so good.

The critical five-second failure is the **dual-audience CTA conflict**: "Explore Businesses" (customer) and "Become a Partner" (business owner) appear side by side in the hero with nearly identical visual weight — same padding, same font size, same rounded corners. A first-time customer sees two equal-weight buttons and does not immediately know which is theirs. This is a classic two-sided marketplace anti-pattern.

Every benchmark platform (Airbnb, OpenTable, Uber Eats) places the business/host CTA in the nav or footer, never competing with the primary customer CTA in the hero.

**Trust within 5 seconds:** The only social proof is a pill badge with "500+ businesses on Savor." This is a rounded, anonymous number. There are no visible logos, no real business names (at this scroll position), no user testimonials, no city names. The trust signal is numerical but not credible.

---

### 2. Information Architecture — The Story Problem

**Current flow reads as:**

> "Here's what Savor is. Here are some numbers. Here are the categories. Here are some businesses. Oh, here are dishes from a restaurant. And here's how it actually works. And here's the business pitch. And here are all the features."

**The story is inverted.** "How It Works" (step 6) should precede "Here Are the Businesses" (step 4). Visitors are shown a product catalogue before they understand the product concept. The Nielsen heuristic of **match between system and real world** requires presenting information in the order users think, not the order content was added.

**More critically:** `FeaturedSection` (step 5) is architecturally wrong on this page. It fetches menu items from the **DEFAULT tenant's API** (`/api/menu/items/?featured=true`) and renders dishes with "Order Now" links routing to `/menu`. On a multi-business marketplace homepage, this section:

1. Shows single-restaurant menu items mid-page
2. Labels them "Featured Dishes" with no restaurant attribution visible
3. Routes "Order Now" to one specific restaurant's menu page
4. Immediately follows "Businesses Near You" (step 4) which showed 6 different businesses

The visitor's mental model collapses: they just saw 6 different businesses, and now they are looking at dishes that appear to belong to no identifiable business, routing them to one restaurant. This makes the entire platform look like a single restaurant app that has been badly reskinned.

**Recommended IA — the correct story:**

```
1. Hero           — Who you are + What you get right now (search)
2. How It Works   — 3 steps: why this is easy (before showing inventory)
3. Categories     — Browse by type (now visitors know how it works)
4. Discovery      — Real businesses on the platform (show, don't tell)
5. Trust strip    — [NEW] Social proof — testimonials / partner logos
6. For Business   — The supply-side pitch (after customer journey is established)
7. Platform Features — Full feature list for evaluators (bottom of funnel)
8. Footer
```

Stats strip: fold into the hero as an inline social proof strip (bottom of hero section), not a standalone section. A standalone stats section interrupts momentum and adds no actionable value.

### 3. Navigation Analysis

**Current nav (post P8.8.7.1):** `Discover | Reservations | For Business`

| Item | Classification | Issue |
|------|---------------|-------|
| Discover | Platform — Marketplace | ✅ Correct primary entry |
| Reservations | Customer — Feature | ⚠️ Feature elevated to product — implies standalone booking app, not a marketplace feature |
| For Business | Business Owner | ✅ Clear after P8.8.7.1 rename |

**"Reservations" as a nav item** suggests users can make a reservation without first selecting a business. On OpenTable, the restaurant discovery page IS the reservation flow. On Savor, `/reservation` is a generic form. Placing this in nav without context creates a "reservation to what?" confusion. The item is not wrong, but it would read better as "Book a Table" (action-oriented) or rolled into Discover with a filter.

**Missing from nav for unauthenticated users:** A sign-in CTA. Currently "Sign In / Get Started" are in the right section of the header but hidden on mobile unless the hamburger menu is opened. For conversion, "Get Started" should remain prominent but "Sign In" could be slightly de-emphasised so it doesn't compete with "Get Started."

### 4. Hero Section Audit

**Headline:** ✅ "Discover / Local Businesses / Near You." — clear, multi-type, marketplace-framed.

**Subheadline:** ⚠️ Opens with "Discover" — repeats the headline's first word verbatim. This wastes the subheadline's unique communication opportunity. The subheadline should add a new layer of information, not paraphrase the headline. It also lists three parallel actions (browse menus, book tables, order) which creates cognitive load — which of these is the primary reason to use Savor?

**CTAs:**
- "Explore Businesses" (orange filled, prominent) — ✅ Correct primary
- "Become a Partner" (glass, equal padding and font weight) — ❌ Wrong audience competing for hero real estate

The "Become a Partner" button is sized and styled to appear as a co-equal action. At a glance, a first-time customer sees two buttons and must evaluate which applies to them. This is friction at the highest-value point of the page. On Airbnb, "Become a Host" is a nav link, never a hero CTA. On OpenTable, restaurant partners are reached via a completely separate nav item ("For Restaurants"). This is industry standard.

**Search:** ✅ Good. Form action goes to `/discover?q=`. Clear placeholder. Search is the fastest path to value for an intent-aware user.

**Category chips:** ✅ Five types, labeled clearly. Provides browsing path for non-search users.

**Missing from hero:**
- No location signal — "Near You" is empty without geolocation. Visitors have no idea where "near you" means.
- No indication of order of magnitude (500+ is a small-sounding marketplace)
- No visual evidence of diversity (one food photo doesn't communicate the breadth from restaurant to food truck)

### 5. CTA Hierarchy — Full Page Analysis

| Section | CTA | Target | Problem |
|---------|-----|--------|---------|
| Hero | "Explore Businesses" | /discover | ✅ Correct primary |
| Hero | "Become a Partner" | /restaurant | ❌ Wrong audience, equal visual weight |
| Stats | — | — | ❌ Orphaned content, no next step |
| Categories | "View all" (text link) | /discover | ❌ Too subtle, loses conversion momentum |
| Discovery | "Discover All Businesses" | /discover | ⚠️ Duplicate of hero primary CTA |
| FeaturedSection | "Order Now" × N | /menu | ❌ Routes to single restaurant. Breaks platform model. |
| HowItWorks | 3 inline links | /discover, /discover, /reservation | ✅ Correct |
| ForBusiness | "Register Your Business" | /register | ✅ Correct |
| ForBusiness | "Learn More" | /restaurant | ✅ Correct |
| ForBusiness | "Admin Preview" | /console | ❌ Admin panel link on public marketing page |

**"Admin Preview" on a public marketing page** exposes the admin panel URL to every visitor. While the panel itself is auth-protected, advertising its existence in a marketing section is a security hygiene and UX anti-pattern. Visitors who click it see an auth wall, not a preview — the label is also misleading.

| Stats | 2 | ❌ Too early — visitors have no context for these numbers yet |

**FeaturedSection:** This is the single most damaging section on the page for marketplace positioning. A visitor who lands on the platform homepage, sees 6 businesses from around the city, then suddenly sees "Featured Dishes" with an "Order Now" button, is rightfully confused. They did not choose a restaurant. Why are they looking at dishes? Where do these come from? This section must be removed from the platform homepage.

### 7. Trust Analysis

**Current trust signals on landing:**

| Signal | Type | Credibility |
|--------|------|-------------|
| "500+ businesses" pill | Number | Low — small, rounded |
| Stats strip 4 numbers | Numbers | Low — obviously mocked (50+, 20+) |
| Discovery cards emoji + gradient | Visual | Very low — looks like wireframe |
| No testimonials | Missing | — |
| No partner logos | Missing | — |
| No press mentions | Missing | — |
| No user-generated content | Missing | — |

**Discovery card visual credibility gap:**

The DiscoveryPreview cards use `emoji + CSS gradient` as the card "photo." No real marketplace (Airbnb, OpenTable, Uber Eats, Google Maps) uses emoji as a primary visual representation of a business. Emoji-based cards signal "prototype" or "demo" to any visitor who has used a real marketplace. This is the most visible trust gap on the page.

The mock business reviews ("4.9 (1,240)") also read as fake because they appear next to emoji cards. Real review counts next to real photos = trust. Exact same numbers next to a `🍽️` emoji = demo data.

### 8. Marketplace vs. Restaurant Positioning

**Elements that correctly position Savor as a marketplace (✅):**
- Hero headline mentioning 5 business types
- 6-card discovery section with mixed types
- "Business Categories" section
- ForBusinessOwners section (supply-side pitch)
- Navigation: Discover → For Business

**Elements that make Savor feel like a single restaurant (❌):**

| Element | Why it signals single-restaurant |
|---------|----------------------------------|
| FeaturedSection | Shows dishes from one restaurant, "Order Now" → `/menu` — the defining page of a single restaurant |
| Stats: "50+ Branches" | Language of a chain restaurant ("we have 50 branches") not a marketplace ("50,000 locations on our platform") |
| "Savor Fine Dining" in DiscoveryPreview | Business name identical to platform name — brand confusion |
| `/menu` links throughout | Single restaurant's menu endpoint used as CTA destination |
| FeaturedSection sub-label "Handpicked" | Without restaurant attribution, these dishes feel like "our restaurant's picks" |

**Verdict:** FeaturedSection is the sole critical offender. Remove it and the positioning becomes clearly marketplace.

### 9. Conversion Friction Map

Path a customer must take to reach Discover:

```
[LAND] → Hero appears (dual CTA confusion — which button is mine?) → [SCROLL]
→ Stats Strip (no CTA — momentum lost) → [SCROLL]
→ Categories (5 links — choice paralysis) → [SCROLL]
→ Discovery Preview (6 more links — information overload) → [SCROLL]
→ Featured Dishes (WAIT — am I on a restaurant page now?) → [SCROLL]
→ How It Works (NOW you explain how it works?) → [SCROLL]
→ For Business Owners (not for me) → [SCROLL]
→ Platform Features (too technical, no action) → [SCROLL]
→ Footer
```

**Friction count before "Discover" CTA is clicked:** 4 competing sections, 1 brand-damaging section, 1 how-it-works that arrived too late, 1 off-target B2B section.

The DiscoveryPreview section does contain "Discover All Businesses" as an orange CTA, but it's the 4th scroll position — many mobile users will never reach it.

### 10. Visual Hierarchy

**Typography scale consistency:**

Every section heading is `text-4xl font-bold` (ForBusinessOwners: same, HowItWorks: same, DiscoveryPreview: same, Categories: same). No section reads as more important than another. On Stripe, section headings scale by importance. On Airbnb, the primary section is visually larger. Here, all 8 sections are typographically equal.

**Section spacing:**

Every section uses `py-20` (80px vertical padding). The monotonous rhythm makes the page feel long without purpose. High-importance sections (Hero, HowItWorks) should have more breathing room; lower-importance sections (Stats, PlatformFeatures) should be more compact.

**Button hierarchy:**

The gap in visual weight between "Explore Businesses" (orange filled) and "Become a Partner" (glass with white border) is insufficient. Both use `px-6 py-3.5`, the same sizing. Visual weight difference should be established by both color AND size to make the primary action undeniable.

**Card density:**

PlatformFeatures: 10 cards in `lg:grid-cols-5` — very dense, reads as a feature checklist. Features should be showcased, not listed. Stripe and Linear use large feature blocks with illustrations or screenshots, not compact icon cards.

### 11. Mobile UX

| Issue | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Hero height `min-h-screen` | On mobile, entire hero fills viewport. First section below fold is stats with no CTA — users who don't scroll see nothing actionable beyond the hero |
| Dual hero CTAs in thumb zone | "Explore Businesses" and "Become a Partner" side by side — easy to tap wrong on mobile |
| Category grid orphan | 5 items in `grid-cols-2` → row of 2 + row of 2 + row of 1 (solo card, full-width) |
| Stats on mobile | 2×2 grid — `grid-cols-2 md:grid-cols-4`. Fine structurally but too early in the flow |
| FeaturedSection cards | 4-column on desktop, 1-column on mobile → 8 cards stacked vertically = very long scroll |
| PlatformFeatures | 2-column on mobile → 10 features = 5 rows → another very long scroll |
| Section spacing | `py-20` (80px) on all sections at mobile width creates very tall content blocks for sparse content (stats, how-it-works) |

### 12. Competitive Benchmark

| Platform | Hero CTA | Business pitch location | How It Works position | Listing card quality | Trust |
|----------|----------|------------------------|----------------------|---------------------|-------|
| **Airbnb** | Search (the CTA IS the search) | Nav link only | Not on landing | Real photos, real names | Host/guest reviews, badges |
| **OpenTable** | Search + date + party size | Footer "For Restaurants" | Position 3 | Real restaurant photos | "X million diners seated" |
| **Uber Eats** | Location input forced | Separate "Uber Eats for Business" domain | Not on landing | Real restaurant photos | "Delivery in X min" live |
| **Booking.com** | Search with dates | Separate tab "List your property" | Not on landing | Real property photos | "Trusted by X travellers" |
| **Savor (current)** | 2 CTAs (customer + partner, equal weight) | Hero CTA + nav + dedicated section | Position 6 (too late) | Emoji + CSS gradients | Mock numbers only |

**What they all do that Savor does not:**
1. The primary CTA IS the search/filter — not a button that navigates to a different page
2. Business partner pitch is a footer link or separate nav — never a hero CTA
3. All listing cards use real photography — no emoji
4. Trust is established through volume + specificity ("43 million nights booked last year") not rounded small numbers ("500+ businesses")
5. How It Works (when present) appears BEFORE the product catalogue
